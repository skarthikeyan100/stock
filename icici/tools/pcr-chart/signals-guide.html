1. Order-book imbalance (futures depth)

What it is. The share of resting orders on the bid side versus the ask side at the best price: (bid qty − ask qty) / (bid qty + ask qty), from tick.bq1 and tick.sq1. Positive means more buyers are waiting, negative means more sellers. The idea is that heavy one-sided depth pushes price the same way.

Illustration: bids on the left, asks on the right. Here 8,060 bid vs 65 ask gives OBI ≈ +0.98, a strong buy-side imbalance.

Reading: no horizon is clearly above 50%. The 30-second, 60-second and 5-minute results all have confidence intervals that cover 50%, and the average move is smaller than the assumed round-trip cost. Depth changes many times a second, so the same depth is counted many times. The effective sample is much smaller than the row count.

2. Futures buildup, with and without PCR

What it is. Futures price and open interest over 5 minutes. Price up with OI up is long buildup (new buyers), price down with OI up is short buildup (new sellers). The PCR test asks whether the PCR move over 15 minutes agrees with the buildup direction.

3. Volatility from VIX (how far, not which way)

What it is. The NIFTY range (high minus low) over the next 15 minutes, compared with the India VIX level and its 15-minute change. VIX is the market's expected volatility, so higher VIX should mean bigger moves.

5. What to do with this

  • Use VIX for sizing and risk, not for direction. It has the most support so far.
  • Keep the PCR-confirmed buildup filter on the watch list. Test it on more days before trading it.
  • Drop order-book imbalance as a direction signal for now. It may still be useful for execution (timing a fill), which we have not tested.
  • Do not read small region cells. Cells with fewer than 50 signals are hidden for that reason.

Cost assumption: 2 index points round trip, used only for the "net" column in the report. Replace it with your actual brokerage and slippage before judging any signal on net return.

Caveats

  • Four sessions. The README asks for 5–10 at minimum, and the region split needs several more to fill.
  • Signals overlap in time, so the row counts overstate the sample. Confidence intervals come from whole-day resampling, which only partly corrects this.
  • Several signals and horizons were tested, so some results will look good by chance.
  • Academic work on intraday predictability is mixed. Order-book imbalance has documented short-horizon power in index futures, but it is regime-dependent and can be spoofed.
