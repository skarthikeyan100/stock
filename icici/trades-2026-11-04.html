  2. Nifty slid from about 23900 to about 23740 through 12:16. CE legs kept being bought at lower strikes, one per 10-point leg, and none of them had yet reached its +10.
  3. 12:16: the last PE sold, the strategy tried to hedge with a CE and found no free strike (all nine strikes it searches were already held). The chain on that side stopped.

Improvements, in order of value

  1. Real risk cap. Hedge buys are exempt from maxInvestment, so open cost reached 176.7k against a 100k cap. Count hedges toward a hard limit (or cap the number of open legs and the hedge depth), and add an aggregate stop on open loss (for example when open unrealized loss exceeds a set share of maxInvestment, close or freeze the chain). Today there is no stop of any kind, and the only thing that bounded the loss was the market turning.
  2. Stop stacking one side in a trend. At 12:16 the book was 10 long CEs and no PEs: a one-way bet at its largest. A rule such as "no new leg on a side that already has N open legs unless Nifty has moved back toward the root" would have limited this.
  3. Hedge search and retry. The hedge looks only at nine strikes around the ATM and skips any already held. When all are held it fails with "No NIFTY CE contract found" and, because the sold leg is closed and no longer receives ticks, never tries again. Widen the window or allow adding to a held strike, and retry on a timer.
  4. Unconfirmed-buy flag (Breeze). The 10:22 hedge buy timed out (the order process waited 90 seconds), then the broker cancelled it with 0 filled. The leg stayed flagged as hedged, so the Breeze chain never continued and its two CE legs ran unhedged for hours. After an unconfirmed buy, ask the broker for the order's final status and clear the flag when it is cancelled with 0 filled, so the hedge is retried.
  5. Double-up flag. A double-up skipped because of the cap still marks the leg as doubled, so it can never double later even if the cap frees. Set the flag only when the double-up actually happens.
  6. Measure the risk. No tick history is stored, so the real worst drawdown and each leg's maximum adverse move cannot be computed after the fact (this report can only use status samples). Log a periodic open-P&L snapshot per instance so the next review has numbers.
  7. Keep manual orders off the strategy's contracts. Your manual Kite sells share contracts with tracked legs: the 24000 CE cost basis was 111.40 in the books against 120.19 at the broker, and a manual sell of the same size as a tracked leg could leave the strategy's resting sell opening a short. Either skip contracts with foreign open orders or alert once when one overlaps.

What the values show:

  • The surplus condition never selected anything today. In all 9 slots that reached the decision, the PE surplus was the larger (1.16 to 2.16 times the CE's); the CE won none. The PE sits at the strike below spot and the CE at the strike above, and the depth there is apparently always heavier on the PE side. So the effective rule was just "Nifty fell since the previous slot".
  • The surplus ratio does not separate winners from the loser. Winners had ratios 2.16, 1.27, 1.22, 1.60, 1.70, 1.72; the 12:40 loser had 1.56, in the middle of them.
  • The price move did. The six winners followed falls of 14.0 to 59.5 points; the loser followed a fall of 1.95. A minimum move of about 10 points would have kept all six winners and skipped the trade that lost 27,378 (kite + prism).

Can ups and downs be added to the conditions? Not as they are logged

  • They have a built-in bias. Downs exceeded ups in 23 of 23 slots, whether Nifty rose or fell. The downs share ranged only from 50.4% to 54.4%. The counts come from delivered, coalesced ticks, so they say little about direction. A rule like "ups > downs means up" would have said "down" every time.
  • They do not track the move. Correlation of (ups − downs) with the slot's Nifty move is 0.005 (n = 23); the downs share against the move is -0.09. Example: at 12:30 Nifty rose 50.45 points yet downs were 51.9%; at 10:20 it fell 59.45 points with downs at 50.9%.
  • Weak hint of reversal. The downs share at a slot against the next slot's move has a correlation of +0.34 (n = 22): more down-ticks, then a slightly higher next move. That is not statistically significant at this sample size (it would need roughly 0.42), so treat it as a question, not a rule.
  • Counts treat a 0.05 tick and a 5-point jump equally. Better candidates, which need data logged first: the sum of up-moves against down-moves (magnitude), the same measure over the last 1 to 2 minutes before the slot, and the move over the last 3 slots instead of one.
Suggested next step (no code changed today). Before changing any condition, log one record per slot: time, Nifty, move, ups, downs, up-move sum, down-move sum, CE and PE surplus, decision, and the option and Nifty price 10 and 20 minutes later. Two weeks of that would let these ideas be tested on real data; today's 23 slots are too few. Meanwhile the evidence supports two small changes: a minimum Nifty move of about 10 points for both strategies, and trying limitOffset: 0 on NiftyChange.
